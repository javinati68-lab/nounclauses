📘 What is a Noun Clause?

A noun clause is a dependent clause (containing its own subject and verb) that functions just like a single noun in a sentence. It can act as a subject, direct object, subject complement, or object of a preposition.

1. Common Noun Clause Markers

Statements: that
Wh- Words: what, whatever, who, whoever, whom, whose, which, whichever, when, where, why, how
Yes/No Questions: whether, if

2. Golden Rule: Statement Word Order

Always use Subject + Verb order inside a noun clause—never question inversion!
✗ Incorrect: I don't know where is she.
✓ Correct: I don't know where she is.

AFFIRMATIVE EXAMPLES
  • • What you decided makes complete sense. (Subject)
  • • She believes that practice improves fluency. (Object)
  • • Whoever finishes early may read quietly. (Subject)
NEGATIVE EXAMPLES
  • • He doesn't understand why the meeting was canceled.
  • • They didn't tell us where they parked the car.
  • • I am not sure whether she will attend today.
INTERROGATIVE EXAMPLES
  • • Do you know when the library closes?
  • • Can you explain how this machine works?
  • • Could you tell me if Mr. Rocha graded our essays?

Landmarks Around the World

Landmarks tell us who we are and where we come from. Millions of travelers visit them every year because they want to see history with their own eyes. Many people believe that the Great Wall of China is the longest human-made structure ever built. Its builders hoped that the wall would protect the northern borders, although historians still debate how effective it was.

Other landmarks show what people can achieve with imagination. Some Parisian artists criticized the Eiffel Tower when it opened in 1889, yet nobody could have predicted that it would become the symbol of France. Today, whoever climbs its stairs can enjoy a view of the entire city. In India, the Taj Mahal reminds visitors how deeply an emperor loved his wife, since he ordered it built in her memory.

Some landmarks are natural wonders. Scientists explain that the Grand Canyon was carved by the Colorado River over millions of years. Visitors often wonder how water alone could create such an enormous space. In Peru, archaeologists still ask why the Incas built Machu Picchu so high in the Andes, and nobody knows for certain.

Protecting these places is a global responsibility. Experts warn that too many tourists can damage fragile sites. What each visitor does matters, so travelers should respect the rules and leave nothing behind. Whether we visit them in person or learn about them online, landmarks remind us that our shared heritage deserves care.
